Lesson 2: Time Expressions

To connect actions in the past, we rely heavily on these specific time words.

1

While / As

Background Action

Used almost exclusively with the Past Continuous to show that an action was in progress.

"While I was walking to work..."
2

When / Suddenly

The Interruption

Used with the Simple Past to introduce a sudden event that interrupts the background action.

"...suddenly, it started to rain."
3

By the time

The Past of the Past

Used to contrast a Simple Past event with something that happened earlier (Past Perfect).

"By the time I arrived, the bus had left."

Lesson 3: The Grammar Timeline

Visualize how the three past tenses interact to form a narrative. Understanding this sequence is key to telling a fluid, native-sounding story.

Past Perfect

The Earliest Action

Think of this as the "past of the past". It describes an action that was completely finished before the main story even began. We use it to provide background context or explain why something happened later in the story.

"I had forgotten my umbrella at home."

Past Continuous

The Background Action

This tense paints the scene. It describes an ongoing, unfinished action that was in progress at a specific time in the past. It sets the mood and creates a background right before a sudden interruption occurs.

"While I was waiting for the bus..."

Simple Past

The Main / Interrupting Event

These are the core sequential events of your story. They are completed actions that push the narrative forward step-by-step. They are also used to describe the sudden events that interrupt the background action.

"...it suddenly started to rain heavily."
